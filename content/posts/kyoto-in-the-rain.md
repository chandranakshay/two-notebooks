We had planned our Kyoto days around a perfect weather forecast. It rained for three of them, and those turned out to be the best days of the trip.

# The crowds simply vanish

The most popular temples in Kyoto can feel like a theme park on a sunny afternoon. In steady rain, most visitors stay inside. We walked the paths of a hillside temple with perhaps a dozen other people, listening to water drip off the roof edges.

# Moss gardens come alive

Kyoto's moss gardens are at their best when wet. The greens become almost luminous, and the stone paths darken to a deep grey. If you only visit one garden in the rain, make it a moss garden.

# Practical things for a rainy day

- Buy a clear umbrella from any convenience store. They're cheap, everywhere, and let you see the view.
- Many temples ask you to take your shoes off. Wear socks you don't mind being seen, and pack a dry spare pair.
- Plan an indoor anchor for the afternoon, such as a covered market or a long, slow lunch.

::: Rain or shine, the quietest time at most temples is the first hour after opening. :::

# Would I go back in rainy season?

Honestly, yes. I'd still pack sunscreen, just in case.
