Everyone told us to ride the famous yellow tram. We never did. Lisbon turned out to be a city best understood at walking pace, one steep cobbled street at a time.

# Day 1: Alfama and the viewpoints

We started at the top and walked down, which is the single best piece of advice I can give anyone visiting Lisbon. The old Alfama district is a tangle of stairways and laundry lines, and every few corners it opens onto a *miradouro*, a viewpoint over the red roofs and the river.

- Go early, before the tour groups arrive.
- Wear shoes with real grip. The stone pavements are polished smooth and slippery, even when dry.
- Let yourself get lost. The whole neighbourhood funnels downhill towards the river eventually.

# Day 2: Belém and far too many pastries

Belém is a long walk along the river, but a flat one. We came for the monastery and stayed for the custard tarts, warm, with cinnamon on top.

::: Tip: the queue for the most famous pastry shop moves faster than it looks, and the seating area inside is much bigger than you'd expect. :::

# Day 3: No plan at all

On our last day we picked a hill we hadn't climbed yet and just walked. We found a tiny café with four tables, a garden we never learned the name of, and a sunset we'll be talking about for years.

> The best day of a trip is often the one you didn't plan.

# What I'd do differently

Book one dinner with live *fado* music in advance. We tried to walk in on a Saturday and every place was full.
